# Code of conduct

## The standard

Be decent to people. Argue with the work, not with the person doing it.

Review here is direct: a refusal names what resolves it, and "this is wrong and here is why" is a
normal thing to read in a pull request. That directness is about the code. It is never a licence to
be unpleasant to the person who wrote it.

## Not acceptable

- Harassment of any kind, in the open or in private, including unwanted contact after being asked to
  stop.
- Insults, demeaning remarks, or comments about somebody's identity or characteristics rather than
  their work.
- Publishing other people's private information.
- Deliberately derailing a discussion.
- Anything a reasonable person would read as an attempt to make somebody unwelcome here.

## Reporting

Report a problem to the maintainers privately, through GitHub's private vulnerability reporting on
this repository: the **Security** tab, **Report a vulnerability**. It is the only private channel this
repository has; say in the title that the report is about conduct rather than a defect.

This is a small project. There is no conduct committee, no appeals body and no promised response time.
A report is read by the people who maintain the repository, which may include the person it is about;
if that is the problem, say so and another way will be found.

## What happens

The maintainers will respond privately. Depending on what happened, that may be a conversation, a
request to change something, an edit or removal of a comment, or a block from the repository. What
was decided, and why, is said.

## Scope

This applies in the issues, pull requests, discussions and commit messages of this repository, and
anywhere somebody is representing it.
